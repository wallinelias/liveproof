## Slide 2 - The problem
**Serves:** Impact & Relevance (45%)
- CVs are written by AI. Screening is increasingly AI too.
- Companies drown in polished, similar, irrelevant approaches.
- Talent get two minutes at a booth to prove themselves.
- Profiling tools add bias and legal risk.

**Speaker note:** The signal at recruiting events is broken. Companies cannot tell people apart, talent cannot show real ability, and AI screening adds risk.

**Visual:** Simple 3-box row with icons or short labels: "Companies: too many similar CVs" / "Talent: 2 minutes to prove it" / "AI screening: bias and legal risk". No screenshot.

## Slide 3 - Idea: work beats CV
**Serves:** Impact & Relevance (45%)
- Companies post a short, real problem from the job.
- Talent solve it live on their phones.
- Companies review the work, not the CV.
- Post, solve, review blind, reveal, follow up.

**Speaker note:** The proof is the work itself. Talent only approach companies whose problem they actually solved, so relevance goes up on both sides.

**Visual:** 5-box flow diagram left to right: Post, Solve, Review blind, Reveal, Follow up. Under the boxes, small labels: "Company", "Talent", "Company", "Talent chooses", "Both".

## Slide 4 - Talent experience
**Serves:** Innovation & UX (35%)
- Join with an event code. No account needed.
- Board shows time box, AI allowed or not, solo or pair.
- Solve against a timer. Same time and tools for everyone.
- AI draft summary is labelled, and talent edits it.

**Speaker note:** Everything is on the phone. Rules are declared up front, and the talent owns their summary and decides when to reveal.

**Visual:** Three phone screenshots side by side from the Talent role: (1) Problem board with badges, (2) Solve screen with timer, (3) AI draft summary or Showcase.

## Slide 5 - Company experience
**Serves:** Innovation & UX (35%)
- Paste a vague need. "Help me write it" drafts a problem.
- Company edits the draft, sets rules, and publishes.
- Blind review: anonymous solver, random order, no scores.
- A human taps "I'm interested". Talent then reveals.

**Speaker note:** Companies often cannot state what they need. The draft helps them, but a human edits, reviews and decides every step.

**Visual:** Two laptop or phone screenshots from the Company role: (1) Post a problem with the AI draft, (2) Blind submissions showing "Solver A7" and the "I'm interested" button.

## Slide 6 - Social layer, kept optional
**Serves:** Innovation & UX (35%)
- Pair rounds let people solve together.
- Partners give skill-tagged endorsements, like "good at debugging".
- Companies see endorsements, never raw chat.
- Solo path is always fully equal.

**Speaker note:** Teamwork is visible but never required, so introverts and extroverts stand on the same footing, and nobody is ranked on endorsements.

**Visual:** One phone screenshot of the Pair round (shared box and chat) next to one of the endorsement view or Showcase with endorsements. Add a small caption "Optional, never required".

## Slide 7 - Responsible by design
**Serves:** Responsible & Feasible (20%)
- AI drafts only. No score, rank, filter or match %.
- Humans make every hiring step. Blind, random order.
- Minimal data: first name and skills, hidden until reveal.
- Avoids high-risk AI screening under the EU AI Act.

**Speaker note:** Our reading of the Act, not legal advice: only two labelled, editable drafting aids, and we would get a legal review before launch. All data is synthetic.

**Visual:** Screenshot of the "Responsible by design" panel in the app (Organiser role). If you cannot find it, use a 3-box diagram: "AI drafts" / "Human decides" / "Talent reveals".

## How to build it in Google Slides
1. Go to slides.google.com, click Blank presentation, then Slide > Change theme and pick a simple one. Set the background to white and use blue (for example #1F5FBF) for titles and shapes.
2. Add 9 slides (Ctrl+M). For each, paste the title, then the 3-4 bullets into the body. Leave the Speaker note in the notes box under the slide.
3. Take phone-size screenshots of the app (browser dev tools, phone view) and insert them with Insert > Image > Upload, as each Visual line says.
4. Build the diagrams with Insert > Shape (rectangles and arrows). Keep fonts large (at least 24 pt) and use at most 4 bullets per slide.
5. Check the file is under 20 MB, then use File > Download > PDF Document (.pdf) and upload that PDF to the Junction form.
